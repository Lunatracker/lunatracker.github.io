Pads vs. Tampons vs. Cups vs. Period Underwear: Which Is Best for Teens?

How each period product works, and how to choose the one that fits you.

2 min read · October 18, 2026

There are more period products than ever, and the choice can feel overwhelming. The good news: there's no wrong answer. Every product on this list is safe for teens when used the right way. The best one is whichever feels most comfortable to you.

Pads

Pads stick to the inside of your underwear and soak up blood. They come in different sizes and thicknesses for light, regular, heavy and overnight flow.

  • Good for: first periods, nighttime, anyone who doesn't want to insert anything
  • Change: every 4 to 6 hours, or sooner if it feels full
  • Downsides: can feel bulky, and not great for swimming

Tampons

Tampons are small cotton plugs that go inside the vagina and soak up blood before it leaves the body. Many come with an applicator to make inserting easier.

  • Good for: swimming, sports, and feeling less bulky
  • Change: every 4 to 8 hours. Never leave one in for longer than 8 hours.
  • Downsides: can take practice. Using the lowest absorbency you need lowers the risk of a rare but serious illness called toxic shock syndrome (TSS).

Yes, teens can use tampons, including with a first period. Starting with a "light" or "slim" size can make it easier.

Menstrual cups and discs

Cups and discs are made of soft, flexible silicone. They sit inside the vagina and collect blood instead of absorbing it. You empty, rinse, and reuse them.

  • Good for: long school days, swimming, and making less waste
  • Change: empty every 8 to 12 hours, depending on flow and the brand's instructions
  • Downsides: there's a learning curve, and you need a sink or a water bottle to rinse it. Teen and small sizes are available.

Period underwear

Period underwear looks like regular underwear, but has absorbent layers built in. You wear it, rinse it, and wash it.

  • Good for: light days, backup against leaks, overnight, and anyone who wants something simple
  • Change: once or twice a day, depending on flow
  • Downsides: you need a few pairs, and you have to carry a used pair home if you change during the day

So which should you choose?

Most people start with pads because they're the easiest to use. As you get more comfortable, you might mix things up, like pads at night, a tampon for swim class, and period underwear on light days. Plenty of people use more than one.

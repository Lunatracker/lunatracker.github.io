How to Talk to Your Parents About Your Period

Easy ways to start the conversation, even if it feels awkward.

2 min read · October 18, 2026

Lots of teens feel embarrassed about telling a parent they got their period, or asking for pads or a doctor's visit. If that's you, you're not alone. The adults in your life have almost certainly dealt with periods themselves or helped someone who has, and most will be glad you came to them.

Why it helps to tell someone

An adult can help you get supplies, show you how to use them, and figure out what's normal. They can also take you to a doctor if something doesn't seem right. You shouldn't have to handle it all on your own.

Ways to start the conversation

You don't need a big speech. Short and simple works:

  • "I think I got my period. Can you help me?"
  • "We're almost out of pads. Can we get more?"
  • "My cramps are really bad. Can we talk about what helps?"
  • "Can I ask you a question about periods?"

Try bringing it up during a calm moment, like in the car or while cooking together. Side-by-side conversations can feel less intense than face-to-face ones.

If talking feels too hard

That's okay too. You could:

  • Write a note or send a text
  • Add pads to the family shopping list
  • Ask another trusted adult, like an aunt, older cousin, coach or school nurse
  • Show them an article (like this one) and say "I have questions about this"

Talking to a dad or male guardian

If you live with your dad or another male guardian, it might feel extra awkward. But plenty of dads help with periods all the time. You can keep it practical: "I need pads" is a perfectly fine sentence. They might be nervous too, and that's okay.

Questions you might want to ask

  • When did you get your first period?
  • What products did you use?
  • Is it normal for my period to be like this?
  • Can I see a doctor about my cramps?

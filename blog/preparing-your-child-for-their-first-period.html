How to Prepare Your Child for Their First Period: A Parent's Guide

When to start the talk, what to say, and what to have ready at home.

2 min read · October 18, 2026

A first period can arrive anywhere from about age 9 to 15. The kids who handle it best are usually the ones who knew what to expect. Here's how to help your child feel prepared, not panicked.

When to start talking

Earlier than you might think. Around age 8 or 9 is a good time to start, even before any signs of puberty. Kids hear things at school, and it's better if their first real information comes from you. Short, ongoing conversations work better than one big talk.

Signs puberty is underway

Most kids get their first period about 2 to 3 years after breast development begins. Other clues include a growth spurt, body hair, and white or clear vaginal discharge, which often appears 6 to 12 months before a first period. Our post on signs a first period is coming covers these in more detail.

What to cover

  • What a period is and why it happens
  • That it's normal, healthy, and happens to about half the world
  • What it might look like (often brown or light at first, not bright red)
  • How to use a pad, and where supplies are kept at home
  • What to do if it starts at school
  • That early periods are usually irregular

If you'd like a script to follow, Luna's Learn lessons are written for kids and can be a good thing to read together.

Put together a first period kit

A small pouch for their backpack takes the fear out of "what if it happens at school?" Include a couple of pads, a panty liner, a spare pair of underwear, and wipes. See our full school period kit checklist.

Tips for dads and single fathers

You don't need to have had a period to be a great support. Keep supplies stocked without being asked, be matter-of-fact, and let your child know they can come to you. If they'd feel more comfortable, help them connect with another trusted adult, like an aunt, grandparent or the school nurse, too.

When to call the doctor

Check in with your child's doctor if:

  • They haven't had a period by 15, or 3 years after breast development started
  • Periods last more than 7 days or are very heavy
  • Cramps keep them home from school
  • Periods stop for 3 months or more after starting

Signs Your First Period Is Coming Soon

The clues your body gives before your first period, and how to be ready.

2 min read · October 18, 2026

There's no way to know the exact day your first period will show up, but your body usually drops some hints. Knowing the signs can help you feel less surprised and more ready when it happens.

What age do most people get their first period?

Most people get their first period between the ages of 10 and 15. The average is around 12, but starting a bit earlier or later is completely normal. Your timing often follows family patterns, so it can help to ask a parent or older relative when theirs started.

Sign 1: Breast development

One of the first signs of puberty is breast budding, which is small, tender bumps under the nipples. A first period usually arrives about 2 to 3 years after breasts start to develop.

Sign 2: Pubic and underarm hair

Hair growing under your arms and in your pubic area is another part of puberty. Pubic hair often appears a bit after breast budding, and periods usually come later on.

Sign 3: A growth spurt

Many people have a big growth spurt before their first period. If you've suddenly grown out of your shoes and jeans, your body is moving through puberty. Growth usually slows down after periods begin.

Sign 4: Vaginal discharge

This is one of the biggest clues. About 6 months to a year before a first period, many people notice white or clear discharge in their underwear. It's totally normal and is your body's way of keeping the vagina healthy. A panty liner can help you feel fresh if there's a lot.

Sign 5: Mood swings, cramps, or acne

As your hormones increase, you might notice feeling more emotional, getting pimples, or having mild cramps or bloating in your lower belly. These can happen in the days before any period, including your first.

How to get ready

You don't have to wait for it to happen to be prepared:

  • Put a pad and a spare pair of underwear in your backpack
  • Learn how to use a pad (check out our period supplies lesson)
  • Talk to a parent, guardian, school nurse or another adult you trust
  • Find out where pads are kept at school

Our lesson on your first period goes through what it actually looks and feels like.

When to check in with a doctor

Talk to a doctor if you haven't had a first period by age 15, or if it has been more than 3 years since your breasts started developing. It's usually nothing to worry about, but a doctor can make sure everything is on track.

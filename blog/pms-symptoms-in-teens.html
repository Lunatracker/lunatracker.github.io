PMS in Teens: Symptoms and What Actually Helps

Why you feel off before your period, and simple ways to feel better.

2 min read · October 18, 2026

Feeling cranky, bloated or breaking out right before your period? That's often PMS, which stands for premenstrual syndrome. It's really common, and there are lots of things that can help.

What is PMS?

PMS is a group of physical and emotional symptoms that show up in the week or two before your period. It's linked to the rise and fall of hormones during your cycle. Symptoms usually get better within a few days after your period starts.

Want to know more about how hormones change during the month? Read how hormones work.

Common PMS symptoms

Physical symptoms:

  • Bloating or feeling puffy
  • Cramps in the lower belly or back
  • Sore or tender breasts
  • Headaches
  • Acne breakouts
  • Feeling extra tired or extra hungry

Emotional symptoms:

  • Mood swings
  • Feeling irritable, sad or anxious
  • Crying more easily
  • Trouble focusing or sleeping

What helps with PMS

  • Move your body. A walk, a bike ride or dancing in your room can ease cramps and lift your mood.
  • Sleep. Aim for 8 to 10 hours a night, especially the week before your period.
  • Eat regular meals. Snacks with protein and fiber can help with energy and cravings.
  • Go easy on salt and caffeine. Salty snacks can make bloating worse, and caffeine can make you feel more on edge.
  • Use heat. A heating pad or warm bath relaxes cramping muscles.
  • Drink water. Staying hydrated can actually help with bloating.

For more ideas, see our lesson on handling cramps.

Track your symptoms

PMS is easier to handle when you can see it coming. If you log your symptoms and moods each day, you might notice that you always get a headache 3 days before your period, or feel down the week before. Knowing that can help you plan ahead and remind yourself that the feeling will pass. Luna lets you log cramps, bloating, headaches, moods and your own custom symptoms right on the calendar.

When to see a doctor

PMS can be uncomfortable, but it shouldn't stop you from living your life. Talk to a doctor if:

  • Your symptoms keep you home from school or activities
  • Your mood changes feel really intense, like deep sadness, hopelessness, or anger you can't control
  • Pain medicine and home remedies don't help your cramps

Some people have a more serious form of PMS called PMDD, and a doctor can help. If you ever have thoughts of hurting yourself, tell a trusted adult right away.

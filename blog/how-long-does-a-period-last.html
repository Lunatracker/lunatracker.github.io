How Long Does a Period Last? A Guide for Teens

What's normal for period length and cycle length when you're new to periods.

3 min read · October 18, 2026

If you've just started your period, one of the first questions you probably have is: how long is this going to last? The short answer is that most periods last between 2 and 7 days. The longer answer is that, for teens, there's a lot of normal variety, and it can take a while for your body to find its rhythm.

How many days is a normal period?

A period (the bleeding part of your cycle) usually lasts 2 to 7 days. Many people bleed for about 5 days. The first day or two are often the heaviest, and the last couple of days can be very light, or just some spotting.

If your period goes longer than 7 days, that's worth mentioning to a parent or doctor, especially if it happens more than once.

Period length vs. cycle length

These two get mixed up a lot, so here's the difference:

  • Period length is how many days you bleed.
  • Cycle length is the number of days from the first day of one period to the first day of the next one.

For adults, a cycle is often around 28 days. For teens, cycles anywhere from about 21 to 45 days apart are considered normal, because your hormones are still settling in.

Why teen periods are so unpredictable

Your period is controlled by hormones like estrogen and progesterone. When you first start, your body is still learning how to make them on a regular schedule. That's why one period might last 3 days and the next one 7, or why you might go two months without a period and then get two close together.

For most people, periods become more regular about 2 to 3 years after the first one. You can read more about this in our lesson on normal teen periods.

How much blood is normal?

It can look like a lot, but most people lose only a few tablespoons of blood over a whole period. Some normal signs of a heavy day are needing to change your pad or tampon every few hours, or seeing small clots.

Signs that your flow might be too heavy include:

  • Soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several hours in a row
  • Needing to double up on pads, or waking up at night to change
  • Clots bigger than a quarter
  • Feeling dizzy, very tired, or out of breath

When to talk to a doctor

Check in with a doctor if:

  • Your period lasts more than 7 days
  • Your periods come less than 21 days or more than 45 days apart
  • You haven't had a period in 3 months after they started
  • Your flow is very heavy (see the list above)
  • Your cramps are so bad that you miss school or activities

None of these automatically means something is wrong. A doctor can help figure out what's going on and how to feel better.

Tracking makes it easier

The best way to learn what's normal for you is to write it down. When you track the first day of each period and how many days it lasts, patterns start to show up, and you'll have real answers if a doctor asks.

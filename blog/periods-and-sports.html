Periods and Sports: Can You Swim, Run and Play on Your Period?

Yes, you can keep playing. Tips for swimming, practice and game day.

2 min read · October 18, 2026

Having your period doesn't mean sitting on the sidelines. You can swim, run, dance, play soccer and do pretty much anything else. In fact, exercise can even help with cramps. Here's how to feel confident on game day.

Can you swim on your period?

Yes. Water pressure can slow your flow while you're swimming, but it doesn't stop it completely, so a pad won't work in the pool. Instead, try:

  • A tampon
  • A menstrual cup or disc
  • Period swimwear for lighter days

If you're not comfortable with any of those yet, it's okay to sit out or wear shorts over your suit. Do what feels right for you.

Choosing products for sports

  • Running and team sports: A tampon or cup stays put during lots of movement. If you use a pad, choose one with wings and wear snug shorts or leggings.
  • Dance and gymnastics: Tampons or cups work well under leotards. Period underwear can be a good backup.
  • Long tournaments: Pack extras and change between games.

Check out our period supplies lesson for how each product works.

Preventing leaks

  • Change your product right before practice or a game
  • Wear dark-colored shorts or leggings
  • Use period underwear or a liner as a backup
  • Keep a spare pair of shorts and underwear in your sports bag

Cramps and exercise

It might feel like the last thing you want to do, but moving your body can actually make cramps feel better. It releases endorphins, your body's natural painkillers. Warm up slowly, stay hydrated, and take it a little easier if you need to. Our lesson on handling cramps has more tips.

A note for serious athletes

Training very hard, especially without eating enough to fuel it, can cause periods to become irregular or stop altogether. That's not a good thing for your body. If you're an athlete and your periods stop for 3 months or more, talk to a doctor or your coach.

Plan around your cycle

Big meet coming up? Tracking your cycle can give you a heads-up about whether your period might fall on the same week, so you can pack the right supplies. Luna's predictions are built for young cycles and get better the more you log.

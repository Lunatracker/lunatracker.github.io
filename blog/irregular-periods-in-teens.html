Irregular Periods in Teens: What's Normal and What's Not

Why your cycle is all over the place, and when that's worth a check-up.

2 min read · October 18, 2026

Your period came three weeks ago, and now it's back? Or it's been two months and nothing? Irregular periods are one of the most common things teens worry about, and in most cases they're a normal part of growing up.

What "irregular" means

A period is called irregular when it doesn't come on a set schedule. For teens, cycles from about 21 to 45 days apart are considered within the normal range, even if they change from month to month. That's a much bigger range than for adults.

Why teen periods are irregular

Your cycle is run by a team of hormones that your brain and ovaries send back and forth. When you first start, that system is still new, and it doesn't always release an egg every month. Without ovulation, your period can come early, late, or skip a month. It can take 2 to 3 years for everything to settle into a pattern.

Our lesson on how hormones work explains this in more detail.

Other things that can affect your cycle

  • Stress, like exams, moving or family changes
  • Big changes in exercise, especially intense training
  • Not eating enough or big changes in weight
  • Being sick
  • Travel and changes in sleep
  • Some medications

When an irregular period is worth checking

Talk to a doctor if:

  • Your periods come less than 21 days apart, or more than 45 days apart, again and again
  • You go 3 months or more without a period after they've started
  • Your period lasts longer than 7 days
  • Your bleeding is very heavy
  • Your periods were regular for a while and then became irregular
  • You have a lot of facial or body hair growth, or bad acne, along with irregular periods

These can sometimes be signs of a condition like PCOS (polycystic ovary syndrome) or a thyroid issue, which a doctor can test for and help with.

Why tracking matters

"Irregular" is hard to describe from memory. If a doctor asks when your last few periods were, a calendar makes it easy to answer. Luna is built for exactly this: its predictions are designed for young cycles that can be anywhere from about 20 to 45 days apart, and they get better as you log.
